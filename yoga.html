Yoga

In my yoga classes, I combine my experience in Hatha Yoga, Yin Yoga and Taoist Yoga.

Depending on the type of class, you may find basic Hatha Yoga postures, flowing yoga sequences, deeply releasing Yin Yoga, clearly guided core-strengthening exercises with a Pilates-inspired approach, and whole-body breathing.

Because of my long experience with Qi Gong, I also incorporate the Five-Element system from Traditional Chinese Medicine into my yoga classes, as well as softer breathing techniques from Daoist practice.

The word yoga means union — a reconnection with something greater than ourselves. You might call it the Tao, God, nature, or simply that which connects everything.

To experience this connection, we first need to become more connected to our own body, not only physically but also energetically.

In practice, yoga becomes a process of letting go of what we no longer need to carry: physical tension, limiting beliefs, habitual patterns and the stress that builds up in our daily lives. As we become quieter and more sensitive, we can begin to notice what lies underneath it all: stillness.

Over time, we develop the capacity to connect with this stillness at any time — not only on the mat, but also in daily life and in our interactions with others. We feel more connected to ourselves and to others, and we can simply be with ourselves and with others. That is practicing “Yoga”.
